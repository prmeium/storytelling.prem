hi, i’m prem.

i love telling stories, getting people in the same room, and helping good ideas find their first believers.

i’m from ahmedabad. usually somewhere between a camera, a conversation, and an idea i can’t leave alone.

here’s how i got here.

i can watch the same few seconds of a video five times.

the curve of a car. the energy in a crowded room. a cut that lands so perfectly you have to go back and see it again.

i want to know why it worked. was it the timing? the frame? the tiny detail i almost missed?

i call that feeling a chef’s kiss. when the whole thing comes together and, for a moment, you forget you’re watching something someone made.

last year, this page was mostly me, a camera, and a plan to tell better stories. i still care about that perfect few seconds.

but i started getting curious about what happened around them.

in college, i kept finding my way into conversations with creators and founders. people with half an idea and enough energy to try it anyway.

i liked that stage of things. when an idea was still a little messy, and the right conversation could move it forward.

so i started the baithak club.

it grew to 200+ creators and founders. we supported eight student ventures along the way. i ran the meetups, the partnerships, and the less photogenic parts: invitations, follow-ups, making sure things actually happened.

there’s a lot of work outside the frame of a good event photo.

that’s where i learned something i still carry with me: getting people to show up is a skill. giving them a reason to come back is the real work.

i’d started out thinking about what made someone watch. now i was thinking about what made them stay.

that curiosity followed me into events, partnerships, and growth.

i did offline acquisition at famapp, then built partner networks at project clay. lots of conversations. lots of follow-ups. plenty of things i had to do badly before i could do them well.

i got used to making the first approach, asking questions, and trying again with a little more understanding than i had the day before.

most recently, i worked on growth and go-to-market at eduro and spurby.

at eduro, i ran creator campaigns and took an ai learning product into a room of about 70 students.

i explained what it could do. then i watched what they actually did with it.

that second part was the interesting one. what felt obvious to me wasn’t always what clicked for them. sometimes the thing they wanted to try was the thing i hadn’t thought to lead with.

spurby put me in a different kind of conversation.

i researched fashion brands, spoke with founders and operators, and tried to understand where work got stuck between teams. the handoffs. the follow-ups. the places where one person was waiting on another.

the work looked different from holding a camera. more research, calls, and trackers. but i kept finding my way back to the same question.

what would make someone actually care?

with a video, it might be a second watch. with a community, another visit. with a product, someone deciding to try it for themselves.

i’d been following that question through different kinds of work without quite knowing how to put it into words.

some things i tried worked. some didn’t. i learned from both, and i’m still learning.

if you want the projects and specifics, here’s my portfolio ↗.

which brings me to now.

i’ve just wrapped up my role at eduro and spurby. i’m looking for my next early-stage team: growth, partnerships, go-to-market, or founder’s office.

i want to talk to users, help shape the story, test ideas, and stick around to see what happens after we ship.

remote works. i can start immediately.

and yes, i still want to make things that earn a second watch.

if you’re building something you care about, i’d like to hear the story.
